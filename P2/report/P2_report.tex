\section{Exercise 1}

\subsection{Question}
Simulation of a simple Lennard-Jones fluid, such as liquid argon, with a hand-made FORTRAN MD code which incorporates the Leap-frog Verlet algorithm.

\paragraph{STEP 1: Understanding the code.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Open the FORTRAN file \texttt{leapfroglj.f} and have a look.
  \item Note that you'll need 2 auxiliary files: (1)~\texttt{leap-lj.data} and (2)~\texttt{leap-conf.data}, containing (1)~data input and (2)~initial coordinates and velocities, respectively.
  \item The main program is ready to simulate a LJ liquid of 500 atoms of mass $39.948\text{ uma}$, $\sigma = 3.405\text{ \AA}$ and $\varepsilon/k_B = 119.8\text{ K}$ (Argon), with a time step of $\Delta t = 0.01\text{ ps}$. Remember that the LJ potential is of the form:
\begin{equation}
  V_{\text{LJ}}(r) = 4\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right].
\end{equation}
  \item Compile the code and make a run of $10000$ time steps. If you compute the temperature $T$ and total energy $E$ (kinetic + potential) you will get the following figures.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Code}
This step runs the leap-frog program supplied with the practice, transcribed to free-form Fortran without algorithmic changes. The full program, with every later addition, is listed in the Anex.

\subsection{Results}
A run of $N_{\text{conf}} = 10000$ steps was performed for $N = 500$ argon atoms interacting through a Lennard-Jones potential truncated at $r_c = 2.5\sigma$, with $\Delta t = 0.01\text{ ps}$. Positions, velocities and energies are stored in Lennard-Jones reduced units. Figure~\ref{fig:ex1} shows the instantaneous total energy $E_{\text{tot}} = E_{\text{kin}} + E_{\text{pot}}$ (without the long-range tail) and the kinetic temperature $T$ along the trajectory.

\subsection{Discussion \& Notes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Method and setup:}
  The integration algorithm is leap-frog Verlet. Velocities are stored at half-steps $t+\Delta t/2$; the on-step velocity used for the kinetic energy is the average of the two neighbouring half-step values. The force is the truncated Lennard-Jones force with cutoff $r_c = 2.5\sigma$ and minimum-image periodic boundaries. The input files fix $N = 500$, $m = 39.948\text{ u}$, $\sigma = 3.405\text{ \AA}$, $\varepsilon/k_B = 119.8\text{ K}$ and $\Delta t = 0.01\text{ ps}$. The cubic box length read from \texttt{leap-conf.data} is $L = 28.694\text{ \AA}$, so
  \begin{equation}
    L^* = \frac{L}{\sigma} = 8.427, \qquad
    \rho^* = \frac{N}{(L^*)^3} = 0.835.
  \end{equation}
  The reduction of time is $\tau = \sigma\sqrt{m/\varepsilon}\,\sqrt{10/R} = 2.156\text{ ps}$, hence $\Delta t^* = 4.637\times 10^{-3}$ and the run covers $t^*_{\max} = 46.37$ ($100\text{ ps}$).

  \item \textbf{Code details:}
  All pair forces and the potential are evaluated in reduced units after subroutine \texttt{reduced} rescales coordinates, velocities, $L$ and $\Delta t$. The temperature uses $n_f = 3N-3 = 1497$ degrees of freedom, removing the three centre-of-mass modes. The NVE energy written to \texttt{energy-leap.dat} is the truncated pair sum $E_{\text{kin}}+E_{\text{pot}}$; the tail correction is introduced only in Exercise~2.

  \item \textbf{Results vs expectations:}
  Leap-frog Verlet is a symplectic, time-reversible integrator, so the truncated energy must oscillate about a constant with no secular drift.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Energy:} $\langle E_{\text{tot}}\rangle = -2266.65$, with a peak-to-peak range of about $4$ reduced energy units ($\min = -2268.56$, $\max = -2264.48$) and $\sigma_E = 0.537$. Relative to $\vert{}E_{\text{tot}}\vert{}$ this is a fluctuation of $0.024\%$. A linear fit of $E_{\text{tot}}(t)$ gives a slope of $-2.7\times 10^{-3}$ per reduced time unit, i.e.\ a change of $-0.13$ over the whole run, well within one standard deviation: there is no measurable energy drift.
    \item \emph{Temperature:} $T$ fluctuates around $\langle T^*\rangle = 0.713$ ($\sigma_T = 0.0175$), corresponding to $T = T^*\,\varepsilon/k_B = 85.4\text{ K}$. Together with $\rho^* = 0.835$, the state point lies in the dense-liquid region of the truncated Lennard-Jones fluid (the lecture RDF/VACF maps at $T^* = 0.8$ span $\rho^* \in [0.764, 0.959]$).
  \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Conclusion:}
  The supplied leap-frog code conserves the truncated NVE energy to better than $0.03\%$ and produces a stable liquid-argon trajectory. This run is the starting point for the structural, thermodynamic and dynamical analyses of Exercises~2 and~3.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion \& Notes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Method and setup:}
  Pair distances are binned into $n_{\text{his}} = 1000$ shells of width $\Delta r = L/(2n_{\text{his}}) = 4.21\times 10^{-3}\sigma$ during the force loop. The RDF in shell $i$ is normalised by the ideal-gas content of the corresponding spherical shell,
  \begin{equation}
    g(r_i) = \frac{\langle n(r_i)\rangle}{N_{\text{conf}}\,N\,\frac{4\pi}{3}\bigl[(i\Delta r)^3-((i-1)\Delta r)^3\bigr]\rho},
  \end{equation}
  counting each pair twice. The pressure uses the atomic virial of the truncated force, $P_{\text{vir}} = (1/(3V))\sum_{i<j}\vec{r}_{ij}\cdot\vec{f}_{ij}$, plus the analytic long-range tail for $g(r)\approx 1$ beyond $r_c$:
  \begin{align}
    E_{\text{tail}} &= \frac{8\pi}{3}\rho N\left(\frac{1}{3r_c^9} - \frac{1}{r_c^3}\right), \\
    P_{\text{tail}} &= \frac{16\pi}{3}\rho^2\left(\frac{2}{3r_c^9} - \frac{1}{r_c^3}\right).
  \end{align}
  With $r_c = 2.5$ these constants evaluate to $E_{\text{tail}} = -223.67$ and $P_{\text{tail}} = -0.7465$.

  \item \textbf{Code details:}
  The histogram and the pair virial are accumulated inside subroutine \texttt{lj}, so every configuration is sampled at no extra pair loop. Distances for $g(r)$ are accepted up to $L/2$ (minimum image), independently of the force cutoff $r_c = 2.5$. The NVE conserved energy remains $E_{\text{kin}}+E_{\text{pot}}$; the thermodynamic totals written to \texttt{thermo-leap.dat} add the tail.

  \item \textbf{Results vs expectations:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{RDF:} $g(r)$ vanishes below the repulsive core ($r \lesssim 0.93$) and develops the oscillations of a dense liquid, in close agreement with the reference plot. The first peak sits at $r = 1.089$ with $g = 3.00$, the first minimum at $r = 1.544$ with $g = 0.576$, and the second and third peaks at $r = 2.079$ ($g = 1.28$) and $r = 2.985$ ($g = 1.11$). At large $r$, $g(r)$ relaxes toward $1$ ($g(L/2) = 0.993$).
    \item \emph{Energy:} The run averages are $\langle E_{\text{kin}}\rangle = 533.66$, $\langle E_{\text{pot}}\rangle = -2800.31$, $E_{\text{tail}} = -223.67$ and $\langle E_{\text{tot}}\rangle = -2490.32$. Kinetic and potential energies fluctuate in anti-phase ($\sigma \approx 13.1$ each) and largely cancel, leaving $\sigma(E_{\text{kin}}+E_{\text{pot}}) = 0.537$ as in Exercise~1. Per particle, $\langle E_{\text{tot}}\rangle/N = -4.981$.
    \item \emph{Pressure:} $\langle P_{\text{kin}}\rangle = 0.594$, $\langle P_{\text{vir}}\rangle = 0.100$, $P_{\text{tail}} = -0.746$ and $\langle P_{\text{tot}}\rangle = -0.052$. The kinetic part is essentially $\rho^* T^* = 0.596$; the large negative tail almost cancels $P_{\text{kin}}+P_{\text{vir}}$, so the total pressure fluctuates around zero ($\sigma_P = 0.120$), as in the reference figure. A slightly negative $P^*$ is consistent with a dense Lennard-Jones liquid close to coexistence at this $T^*$.
    \item \emph{Convergence:} After $10\%$ of the run ($t^* = 4.64$) the running means are already $\langle E_{\text{tot}}\rangle = -2490.53$ and $\langle P_{\text{tot}}\rangle = -0.078$. By $t^* = 23.2$ (half the trajectory) they have settled at $-2490.27$ and $-0.047$, and the final values ($-2490.32$, $-0.052$) change only in the second decimal of $P$ and the first decimal of $E$. Energy is converged to better than $0.01\%$ once a few reduced time units have been averaged; the pressure, being a difference of large opposite contributions, needs of order $t^* \sim 15$--$20$ before the running mean is stable to $\sim 0.01$.
  \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Conclusion:}
  The $10000$-step run is long enough for both $E_{\text{tot}}$ and $P_{\text{tot}}$. The tail corrections are essential: omitting them would shift the energy by $224$ reduced units and the pressure by $0.75$, turning a near-zero pressure into a spuriously positive $P^* \approx 0.69$. The RDF matches the liquid-argon reference curve peak by peak.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Question}
\paragraph{STEP 3: Analysing the dynamics.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Modify the code to calculate the velocity autocorrelation function (VACF) and the mean square displacement (MSD):
\begin{equation}
  C(t) = \bigl\langle \vec{v}(t)\cdot\vec{v}(0)\bigr\rangle,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  r^{2}(t) = \bigl\langle \bigl[\vec{r}(t)-\vec{r}(0)\bigr]^{2}\bigr\rangle.
\end{equation}
  Compute the VACF and the MSD for a run of $10000$ steps. Plot the results.
  \item Write a code to calculate the spectral density (SDW) of the VACF:
\begin{equation}
  S(\omega) = \int_0^{\infty} dt\, C(t)\cos(\omega t).
  \label{eq:sdw}
\end{equation}
  Use a Simpson $3/8$ rule or similar. Plot the results.
  \item Use Einstein's diffusion formula to obtain the self-diffusion coefficient $D$:
\begin{equation}
  D = \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{6t}\, r^{2}(t).
  \label{eq:einstein}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion \& Notes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Method and setup:}
  Following the lecture SAMPLE scheme, a new time origin is stored every $5$ steps in a circular buffer. Positions used for the MSD are unwrapped (the leap-frog update is unfolded by the minimum-image displacement of each step) so that $r^{2}(t)$ is not folded back by the periodic box. The correlation window $n_{\text{cor}} = 1074$ covers $t^* = 4.98$, long enough for $C(t)$ to vanish and for $r^{2}(t)$ to be linear. The spectral density~\eqref{eq:sdw} is evaluated with the composite Simpson $3/8$ rule on the decaying part of $C(t)$ (the first quarter of the window, $t^* \lesssim 1.24$, beyond which $C(t)\approx 0$). Einstein's $D$ is the slope of $r^{2}(t)$ over the last three quarters of the window, discarding the ballistic rise; as a cross-check we also compute the Green--Kubo integral $D = (1/3)\int_0^{t_{\max}} C(t)\,dt$ with the trapezoidal rule.

  \item \textbf{Code details:}
  Subroutine \texttt{sample} has three switches (initialise / accumulate / normalise), as in Frenkel \& Smit. The MSD must not see periodic wrapping: \texttt{runw} holds the continuous trajectories. $n_{\text{cor}}$ is forced to be a multiple of $6$ so that every Simpson $3/8$ panel has three sub-intervals. The intercept of the linear fit is stored in \texttt{yint} because an implicit-typing name \texttt{intercept} would be an integer.

  \item \textbf{Results vs expectations:}
  The four curves reproduce the reference plots for liquid argon.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{VACF:} $C(0) = 2.138$, in agreement with the equilibrium identity $C(0) = \langle v^2\rangle = 3T^*$ ($3\langle T^*\rangle = 2.139$ in reduced units where $m = 1$). After a fast decay the function crosses zero at $t^* = 0.139$ and reaches a backscattering minimum $C = -0.256$ at $t^* = 0.195$. This negative lobe is the signature of a dense liquid: particles rebound from their first neighbour shell (the RDF peak at $r = 1.09$). By $t^* = 0.65$ one has $\vert{}C\vert{} < 0.02$, and $C(t)$ then fluctuates around zero ($C(t_{\max}) = 0.007$), matching both the reference VACF and the lecture curves at $\rho^* \approx 0.84$, $T^* \approx 0.8$.
    \item \emph{MSD:} $r^{2}(t)$ is quadratic at very short times (ballistic $r^{2} \approx \langle v^2\rangle t^2$) and becomes linear for $t^* \gtrsim 1$. At the end of the window, $r^{2}(4.98) = 1.016$. The fit $r^{2} = 6Dt + b$ on $t^* \in [1.24, 4.98]$ gives $D^* = 0.03313$ (slope $0.1988$, intercept $0.0254$).
    \item \emph{Spectral density:} $S(\omega)$ peaks at $\omega = 8$ with $S = 0.158$ ($S(0) = 0.100$) and has decayed to $5.4\times 10^{-3}$ by $\omega = 40$ and to $5\times 10^{-5}$ by $\omega = 100$, in quantitative agreement with the reference SDW.
    \item \emph{Self-diffusion:} $D(t) = r^{2}(t)/(6t)$ overshoots to $0.0412$ at $t^* = 0.31$ (end of the ballistic/cage regime) and then decreases toward the Einstein value. The fitted $D^* = 0.03313$ and the Green--Kubo estimate $D^*_{\text{GK}} = 0.03358$ differ by $1.4\%$. Converting with $\sigma^2/\tau = 5.377\text{ \AA}^2\text{ ps}^{-1}$ yields
    \begin{equation}
      D = D^*\frac{\sigma^2}{\tau} = 0.178\text{ \AA}^2\text{ ps}^{-1}.
    \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Conclusion:}
  A correlation window of $5$ reduced time units is sufficient: the VACF has died, the MSD is linear, and Einstein and Green--Kubo agree to about one percent. The negative VACF lobe and $D^* = 0.033$ confirm that the simulated state point is a dense Lennard-Jones liquid, consistent with the RDF of Exercise~2 and with the reference figures.
\end{itemize}
